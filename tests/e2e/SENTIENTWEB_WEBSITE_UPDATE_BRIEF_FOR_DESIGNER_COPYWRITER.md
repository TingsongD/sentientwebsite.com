# SentientWeb Website Update Brief for Front-End Designer and Copywriter

Last updated: May 7, 2026

## Executive Direction

SentientWeb should be positioned as:

> Revenue recovery for moments where intent already exists.

The website should not present SentientWeb as a broad top-to-bottom automation suite. The public story should be narrow, credible, and easy for a small B2B SaaS team to understand:

> SentientWeb recovers SaaS revenue when buyers, prospects, or customers already showed intent, then went quiet.

The first public wedge remains:

> Demo Recovery for B2B SaaS teams using HubSpot and Calendly.

The second wedge should be visible, but framed as a private pilot:

> Failed Payment Recovery for Stripe SaaS teams.

This lets SentientWeb tell a broader revenue recovery story without sounding like a generic AI ops platform.

## Core Positioning

### One-Liner

SentientWeb recovers SaaS revenue at the moments buyers and customers go quiet.

### Expanded One-Liner

SentientWeb gives high-intent SaaS visitors an instant AI demo preview, converts peak interest into qualified booked demos, and privately pilots failed-payment recovery for Stripe teams before failed charges become silent churn.

### Primary Public Offer

> Recover demo-ready visitors before they disappear.

SentientWeb engages visitors on pricing, demo, comparison, security, docs, and integration pages, qualifies fit, opens the booking path, and syncs the full context into HubSpot.

### Private Pilot Offer

> Stop failed Stripe charges from becoming silent churn.

When a Stripe payment fails, SentientWeb classifies the reason, routes the account by risk, sends approved recovery paths for low-risk cases, creates owner tasks for high-value or ambiguous accounts, retries when appropriate, and logs every save or write-off in CRM.

## Target ICP

### Best First Audience

Small B2B SaaS companies with:

- $10k-$100k MRR for the failed-payment pilot, or $1M-$20M ARR for demo recovery.
- Stripe Billing for subscription payments.
- HubSpot as CRM or marketing system.
- Calendly, Chili Piper, or simple calendar booking flow.
- Visible pricing, demo, comparison, docs, security, or integration pages.
- Founder, Head of Growth, VP Sales, RevOps, CS, or Finance Ops owner reachable.
- Enough high-intent traffic or failed payments that the weekly leak is visible.

### Avoid Early

- Very low-ARPU consumer subscriptions.
- Regulated intake: healthcare, legal, lending, insurance, employment, education.
- Companies needing SOC 2 Type II before any pilot.
- Broad ecommerce/cart recovery.
- Zero-traffic startups that need demand generation, not recovery.

### 4. Failed Payment Recovery Section

This should not be above Demo Recovery. It should appear as a private pilot or secondary module.

Headline:

> Private Stripe pilot: recover failed payments before they become silent churn.

Body:

> Stripe tells you a payment failed. SentientWeb decides whether it needs a card update path, SCA fix, retry, billing-owner task, save call, or write-off, then logs the outcome.

Workflow:

> failed payment -> classify reason -> route by risk -> auto-send approved fix for low-risk cases -> assign owner for high-value or ambiguous cases -> retry -> log save/write-off in CRM

Client-safe rules:

- Low-risk, clear-reason failures can be automated.
- High-value accounts get an owner task.
- Ambiguous, disputed, canceled, or angry accounts do not receive automated recovery messages.
- Customer-facing messages use approved templates, not free-form AI.
- Every retry, save, reply, write-off, and owner action gets logged.

## Design Guidance

### Overall Direction

The site should feel like a focused SaaS revenue tool, not a marketing agency, generic AI bot, or futuristic demo toy.

Keep the current black/neon visual identity, but reduce language that feels theatrical. Use visual hierarchy to show maturity levels:

- Public wedge: stronger visual emphasis.
- Private pilot: visible but secondary.
- Add-ons/reporting: lower emphasis.

### Use-Case Cards

Each use-case card should show:

- Status pill.
- Use-case name.
- One-sentence outcome.
- 3 concrete bullets.
- CTA.

Status pills should make maturity clear:

- Public wedge
- Private pilot
- Pilot add-on
- Reporting layer

### Visual Metaphor

Do not overuse "digital plumbing." It can appear lower on the page as a brand analogy, but the top of the site should be concrete:

> buyers and customers go quiet

> recover demo-ready visitors

> failed Stripe charges become silent churn

## Acceptance Criteria

The updated website should pass these checks:

- A first-time visitor can explain SentientWeb in one sentence.
- The homepage clearly says Demo Recovery is the public wedge.
- Failed Payment Recovery is visible but clearly labeled as private pilot.
- The nav says "Use cases," not "Solutions."
- The site does not imply all use cases are fully launched.
- The site does not claim autonomous customer messaging without approval rules.
- The pricing page sells a 30-day pilot, not a broad platform.
- Vertical clutter is removed from nav/footer.
- The copy avoids "AI chatbot" positioning.
- The copy does not promise guaranteed recovery.
